# PRC-007 — Emergency Procurement

> **SYNTHETIC DEMO CONTENT — This document, company, people, limits, vendors and procedures are fictional. It is designed only to test the PIPPA knowledge-assistant prototype and must not be used as legal, financial, safety or operational advice.**

## Control information

- **Owner:** Chief Procurement Officer
- **Audience:** Operational leaders, procurement and finance
- **Status:** CURRENT
- **Version:** 1.8
- **Effective:** 04 September 2026
- **Review due:** 01 March 2027

## When to use this document

An urgent event threatens safety, business continuity, critical assets or customer service and normal sourcing time is unavailable.

## Authoritative policy clauses

### PRC-007-5.1

> Emergency procurement is limited to the minimum goods or services needed to stabilise an immediate safety or continuity risk.

### PRC-007-5.2

> Urgency caused only by poor planning does not qualify unless delay would now create a greater business risk.

### PRC-007-5.3

> When approval systems are unavailable, the required approvers may give recorded verbal authority for the minimum emergency commitment. That authority must be documented within one business day and does not replace a mandatory written contract, call-off, insurance, safety or data control before goods, customer data or regulated work are transferred.

### PRC-007-5.4

> Due diligence, conflict checks, insurance and a written scope remain mandatory, but may be sequenced on an accelerated basis.

### PRC-007-5.5

> Emergency authority is based on total expected value: Director plus Procurement Manager plus Finance Manager up to AED 50,000; VP/COO plus CPO plus Finance Controller for AED 50,001–250,000; and CFO plus CEO above AED 250,000.

### PRC-007-5.6

> Missing the one-business-day deadline does not invalidate goods or services already accepted to protect life or stabilise continuity, but it is a control breach and does not authorise further work beyond safe stabilisation. On discovery, stop non-essential extension, raise a Late Emergency Approval Regularisation within one business day, preserve the original timestamped verbal record, and obtain the original emergency approvers plus Procurement Governance Manager and Finance Controller review. If no approved PO existed before commitment, PRC-004-4.6 also applies. If the required written call-off, insurance, safety or data control was absent before mobilisation, escalate immediately to Legal and the relevant risk owner; retrospective paperwork cannot represent that the pre-mobilisation control existed.

## Procedure

1. Describe the immediate risk and minimum stabilising need.
2. Contact Procurement and confirm whether an approved vendor can respond.
3. Obtain emergency approval at the total expected value; recorded verbal authority is permitted only if approval systems are unavailable.
4. Before goods, customer data or regulated work are transferred, execute the minimum required written instrument, such as an emergency one-page call-off, and confirm insurance, safety and data controls.
5. Create the full PO/contract and capture the verbal approval within one business day.
6. If that deadline is missed, stop non-essential extension and complete PRC-007-5.6 plus PRC-004-4.6 when no approved PO existed; never backdate either record.
7. Complete a post-event review within five business days.

## Approval and decision authority

- Director plus Procurement Manager plus Finance Manager: up to and including AED 50,000.
- VP/COO plus CPO plus Finance Controller: AED 50,001–250,000.
- CFO plus CEO: above AED 250,000.
- Late documentation: original emergency approvers plus Procurement Governance Manager and Finance Controller; PRC-004 approvals are cumulative when the commitment also lacked an approved PO.

## Required records

- Emergency justification
- Vendor check
- Original approval timestamp and recording
- Scope
- Written pre-mobilisation controls
- PO/contract
- Late Emergency Approval Regularisation where required
- No-PO Exception where separately required
- Post-event review

## Escalation rule

Escalate any personal conflict, cash request, unsafe vendor or inability to define the minimum scope.

## Do not

- Do not label routine urgency as an emergency.
- Do not omit price reasonableness or conflict checks.
- Do not extend emergency work beyond stabilisation without normal approval.
