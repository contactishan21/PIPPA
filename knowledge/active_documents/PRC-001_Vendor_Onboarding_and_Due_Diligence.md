# PRC-001 — Vendor Onboarding and Due Diligence

> **SYNTHETIC DEMO CONTENT — This document, company, people, limits, vendors and procedures are fictional. It is designed only to test the PIPPA knowledge-assistant prototype and must not be used as legal, financial, safety or operational advice.**

## Control information

- **Owner:** Chief Procurement Officer
- **Audience:** Requestors, buyers, vendor managers and approvers
- **Status:** CURRENT
- **Version:** 1.9
- **Effective:** 04 September 2026
- **Review due:** 01 March 2027

## When to use this document

A business team wants to engage a new vendor or reactivate an inactive vendor.

## Authoritative policy clauses

### PRC-001-4.1

> No vendor may receive a purchase order or perform work until required due diligence, bank verification, tax documentation and sanctions screening are complete.

### PRC-001-4.2

> The business requestor must document the need and must not conduct due diligence on their own preferred vendor.

### PRC-001-4.3

> Bank-account details must be verified through an independent callback using a trusted contact source.

### PRC-001-4.4

> Higher-risk services require Legal, Information Security, HSE or Data Privacy review as applicable.

### PRC-001-4.5

> Vendor-onboarding and single-source-route approval determine vendor eligibility only. They do not approve any commitment or contract value; the resulting spend must separately follow FIN-001.

### PRC-001-4.6

> A vendor requires specialist risk review when its scope includes personal or payment data—Privacy and Information Security; system access—Information Security; on-site or hazardous work—HSE; food or product safety—Quality; vehicles—Transport Risk and HSE; regulated activity—Legal or Compliance; subcontracting or intellectual property transfer—Legal; or business-critical continuity—Business Continuity and the operational owner. Procurement records every triggered review and owner; absence of a trigger must also be recorded rather than assumed.

### PRC-001-4.7

> Before activation, Procurement must obtain and verify the vendor's current trade licence or equivalent registration, tax-registration evidence where applicable, and bank-account evidence through the approved due-diligence record.

### PRC-001-4.8

> Denied-party, sanctions and required compliance screening must be completed before activation. A failed or uncertain result is held and escalated to Compliance; it cannot be overridden by the requestor.

### PRC-001-4.9

> Vendor onboarding confirms eligibility only. It does not approve a purchase order, contract, spend or bank-detail change; those require their own controlling approvals, including PRC-003 and FIN-001 where applicable.

## Procedure

1. Submit business need, scope and estimated annual spend.
2. Procurement checks existing approved vendors first.
3. Classify the scope against every trigger in PRC-001-4.6 and assign each specialist review or record that no trigger applies.
4. Collect vendor registration and risk documents through the approved portal.
5. Complete independent bank verification and required risk reviews.
6. Approve vendor master creation and issue the vendor ID before ordering.

## Approval and decision authority

- Procurement Manager: standard vendor-risk onboarding; this approval creates vendor eligibility and does not approve spend.
- CPO: high-risk, annual-spend-above-AED-250,000 or single-source onboarding route; this still does not approve spend.
- FIN-001 approvers: every resulting commitment at its total expected value.
- Specialist functions: risk-specific approval.

## Required records

- Business justification
- Due-diligence pack
- Bank callback
- Risk approvals
- Vendor ID

## Escalation rule

Escalate ownership opacity, adverse screening, bank-detail pressure or refusal to provide required documents.

## Do not

- Do not accept bank details only by email.
- Do not split spend to avoid review.
- Do not allow work to begin on a pending vendor.
